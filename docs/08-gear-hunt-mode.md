# 08: Gear Hunt (co-op mode without saboteurs)

Working title: **Gear Hunt**. A co-op variant of the same round (pages, bins, inspector, Done button) with nobody to catch. Instead, the job site is in a bad state when the round starts: everything looks grey, loose bricks lie everywhere, the house is dark, half the cupboards are padlocked and the dog is in a mood. Each of these troubles has exactly one piece of **gear** hidden in the map that fixes it for whoever wears it. Gear is worn, not consumed, and a player can wear all of it at once, so one person alone can finish the job. With a group, who wears what becomes the game.

The lobby's **Mode** setting picks it: Saboteur (the ordinary round), Gear Hunt, or plain co-op for people who just want to build. The demo panel has the same choice.

## What is built

The first version has all six pieces of gear, the padlocks, the litter, the dead panel, the leashed dog and the heavy build, the mode in the lobby and the demo panel, the gear row in the HUD, worn gear on the builders and padlocks on the cupboards. Not yet built, in roughly the order they are worth doing: per-trouble checkboxes and a gear hiding difficulty in the lobby (every trouble is on and a third of the gear hides in closed places), the dog running off with dropped gear, a "wore it longest" line on the results screen, the walkie-talkies and the wheelbarrow. Playtests decide the litter amount and the extra time first.

## Design rules

1. **One of each.** Every piece of gear exists once per round. Sharing is physical: take it off, hand it over, walk it across the map.
2. **Stack everything.** No slots, no weight. A player wearing the glasses, the boots, the headlamp and the key ring at once is the solo game.
3. **Solvable without any gear.** Finding gear must never require gear. The only hard locks in the mode (padlocks, darkness) never stand between the player and the item that opens them. Everything else is a slowdown, not a wall.
4. **Legible trouble.** When something is wrong, the HUD says what and names the gear for it ("Too dark to read. The headlamp is somewhere outside"). The player should never wonder whether it is a bug.
5. **Visible gear.** Worn gear shows on the builder (goggles, yellow boots, a lamp on the hat, keys on the belt), so a glance tells who to ask.

The goggles are the heart of the mode. In a group, the goggle wearer is the only one who can read the manual's colours, so they either do all the fetching and checking themselves, or call colours out for the others: which bin to take from ("the 2x4 bin on the top shelf, that one is dark red"), what colour someone is holding, and where it goes on the build. The others build by shape and position and trust the caller. Solo, the moment you find the goggles the grey world snaps into colour, which is the best feeling the mode has, so they should not be the first thing found: see "Hiding gear".

Two more that fit the rules, for after the first version:

- **Walkie-talkies** (a pair, the only item that exists twice): the two holders hear each other anywhere, past the 12 m chat and 22 m voice range. Only spawns with two or more players.
- **Wheelbarrow**: carries up to a dozen loose bricks, or the build, pushed rather than held. Without it, bricks travel one at a time as now. Needs physics work of its own.

## Round flow

```
Lobby -> Gear reveal (5 s) -> Build phase (12-16 min) -> Final check -> Results -> Lobby
```

1. **Lobby:** the host picks the build, the round length and the mode. In Gear Hunt the host ticks which troubles are on (all by default) and how gear hides: **Easy** (all on open surfaces), **Normal** (a third in hiding places) or **Hard** (half, like pages).
2. **Gear reveal:** instead of a role, everyone sees the list of troubles and the gear for them, so they know what to look for.
3. **Build phase:** as the ordinary round. Gear is clicked to pick up, and goes on at once. The number keys 1 to 6, free because there are no saboteur tools, take off the matching piece in the HUD gear row and drop it at your feet. A dropped piece lies on the floor like a brick and can be picked up by anyone, or by the dog.
4. **No bell.** There are no meetings and no votes in this mode, and the bell is not on the job site. Handing gear over means walking it over.
5. **End:** Done pressed twice, or time out.
6. **Results:** the build next to the target as now, plus time left and gear found, and a line per player of what they wore longest.

**Winning:** the team wins by handing in a correct build before time runs out. Otherwise nobody wins. There is no penalty and no "sent home".

**Timer:** the co-op default is 12 min plus 1 min per trouble that is on, because the first two or three minutes go into the gear hunt. Group size does not change it: more players find gear faster but also need to pass it around.

## Hiding gear

Gear hides in the same pool of spots as pages (open surfaces and closed hiding places), drawn after the pages, with these constraints checked when the round is laid out:

- The key ring never goes into a padlocked hiding place (padlocks are chosen first, then the key avoids them).
- The headlamp never goes into the house or the basement.
- Nothing hides in the dog's mouth or on the floor at the start; gear spawns on surfaces and in hiding places only, so only a dropped piece can be stolen.
- Gear in hiding places follows the lobby setting (Easy: none; Normal: a third; Hard: half).
- Solo and pairs are nudged toward a good order: the goggles never take an open surface within 10 m of the spawn, so the first find is usually boots, a key or the lamp.

A unit test lays out a few hundred seeded rounds and checks every constraint, plus the real solvability property: from the spawn, every piece of gear is reachable without opening a padlock or reading in the dark. Pages carry no such guarantee on purpose: a page in a padlocked locker in the dark basement is the puzzle, and the gear is the answer.

## Open questions

- **Should the goggles grey the whole world?** A full-screen pass is simpler than touching every material, but it also greys the house and the other builders. Greying bricks, bins, marks and the printed pages keeps the house pretty and the trouble clearly about bricks.
- **Does the lamp need to light the server's view of anything?** No, as long as the mode stays co-op. If a saboteur variant of Gear Hunt ever happens (a saboteur hiding gear instead of pages), reading has to move to the server.
- **Litter and the broom.** Sweeping 60 bricks into piles is a real alternative to the boots, and a job someone enjoys. Make sure the litter count is low enough that the broom is a choice, not a chore.
